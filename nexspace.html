NEXSPACE — A Virtual Reality Experience

An immersive Meta Quest virtual reality experience developed in Unity using C#. I contributed as the C# Developer, Creative Director and Asset Manager, building interactive gameplay mechanics, directing the visual identity and optimising 3D assets to deliver a seamless, immersive VR experience..

PROJECT TYPE Group Virtual Reality Project (8 Members)
TIMELINE 6 Months · January – June 2026
ROLE C# Developer · Creative Director · Asset Manager
TOOLS Unity · C# · Meta Quest · Blender
PROGRAMME European Project Semester
RESEARCH PARTNER AP Immersive Lab

Context

Our digital lives have an environmental cost that is easy to overlook. Every search, stream, upload and stored file contributes to a growing digital infrastructure, yet the physical consequences of our online habits often remain invisible.

Developed over six months as part of the European Project Semester at AP Hogeschool in Antwerp, NexSpace was a collaborative VR project created by an international team of eight students in partnership with AP Immersive Lab. Our brief challenged us to explore embodiment and presence in virtual reality through an original immersive experience. We chose data waste as our theme, using environmental storytelling to turn an abstract sustainability issue into a world users could experience for themselves.

The Problem

I was interested in how we could communicate a problem that most people rarely think about. Unlike physical waste, data waste is difficult to see or connect to our everyday behaviour. We wanted users to look beyond their screens and consider what an increasingly digital future might cost the environment.

Our design challenge was to create more than an educational experience that simply presented facts. We wanted to use virtual reality to make the issue feel tangible, allowing users to become part of the environment rather than observe it from the outside. This meant thinking carefully about how embodiment, physical presence and interaction could influence the way people experienced our narrative.

Our design brief: How might we use embodiment and immersive storytelling to make the invisible environmental consequences of data waste tangible, encouraging users to reflect on their everyday digital habits?

Research

Before developing NexSpace, our team explored existing virtual reality experiences at AP Immersive Lab to understand what made an environment feel believable and immersive. I documented my observations of the interactions, environments and storytelling techniques that strengthened or interrupted the feeling of being present in a virtual world.

A central part of our research was embodiment: exploring how users perceive and connect with their virtual bodies and how physical interactions can strengthen their sense of presence. We discussed ideas such as synchronising physical and virtual movements, representing the user's body within the experience and creating objects that responded naturally to touch.

These explorations helped me think beyond the visual quality of a VR environment. I began considering how the user's physical position, the pacing of interactions and the relationship between the real and virtual worlds could influence immersion. We carried these considerations into our prototypes and refined the experience through iterative testing with more than thirty participants across four testing sessions.

We conducted three rounds of user testing with approximately 20 participants per session, recruiting our erasmus cohort to key in. This assisted us in evaluating different aspects of the NexSpace experience, including immersion, embodiment and narration. Each round helped us understand how users connected with their virtual bodies, responded to the environment and emotionally engaged with the story.

Our findings highlighted opportunities to strengthen embodiment through more natural body representation, while 95% of participants felt that the environmental portrayal of data waste made the topic feel real. Testing the narrator voices also revealed how tone and delivery influenced emotional impact, helping us refine the experience based on participant feedback.

85% of participants rated their sense of ownership over the virtual body at 3 or 4 out of 5. This highlighted the potential to strengthen embodiment through more natural body representation and interaction.
95% felt that the environmental portrayal of data waste made the topic feel real. This supported our use of environmental storytelling to communicate an otherwise invisible digital sustainability issue.
86% rated the female narrator's emotional impact at 4 or higher out of 7. The voice comparison revealed how tone and delivery influenced the emotional experience of the narrative.

Solution

We created NexSpace, an immersive Meta Quest experience that explores data waste through environmental storytelling rather than relying on lengthy explanations. I helped shape the creative direction and translate our shared vision into an interactive world, combining visual design, environmental details and gameplay mechanics to communicate the consequences of excessive digital consumption.

A World That Tells the Story: The experience begins in a futuristic city, where users sit inside a moving vehicle as an alien robotic narrator introduces their surroundings. As the journey progresses, the environment gradually deteriorates, allowing users to discover the consequences of data waste through the world itself rather than through direct instruction.

Designing for Embodiment: We explored how physical positioning, interactive objects and the relationship between the user's real and virtual body could strengthen presence. I considered how each interaction fitted into the wider experience, helping the environment feel intentional rather than like a collection of disconnected VR mechanics.

From Reflection to Collapse: The journey continues into a television-filled room, encouraging users to reflect on their own digital habits, before the virtual world collapses into an empty white space. This final transition provides a visual metaphor for the consequences of unchecked digital growth, leaving users with a moment to process the experience.

I contributed to the development in Unity using C#, alongside creative direction, asset management and collaborative world-building. Working with the team, I helped bring the experience together as a cohesive narrative, balancing technical implementation with the visual and interactive decisions that supported our central message.

Reflection

immersive experience involves much more than building a convincing 3D environment. I had to think about how users would experience the world physically, how the narrative unfolded around them and how small design decisions could strengthen or interrupt their sense of presence. Working with an international team also taught me how to bring different creative perspectives together into one cohesive experience.

If there was something I could improve or do differently, I would explore embodiment earlier through more focused prototypes and structured testing. I would also investigate how individual interactions affected users' understanding of data waste, rather than evaluating immersion and storytelling primarily as parts of the overall experience. This could help us better understand which design decisions had the greatest impact.

Overall, NexSpace challenged me to think beyond conventional interface design and consider how people interact with technology through their bodies, surroundings and emotions. It strengthened my skills in Unity and C#, but more importantly, it changed how I approach interactive experiences: starting with what I want users to feel, understand and take away, then designing the technology around that intention.
