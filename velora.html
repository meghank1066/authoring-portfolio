Dublin Bikes - Mobile App Redesign

Redesigning the urban cycling experience through real-time station information, intuitive route planning and user-centred mobile design.

ROLE Individual Project - Frontend Mobile Development
TIMELINE March 2026 - May 2026
Focus Swift · UI/UX · Accessibility · Responsive Design
TECH STACK Swift, SwiftUI, UIKit, Core Data, REST APIs, Figma

Context

Everyone knows that Dublin's public bike-sharing network can be challenging, especially when users need to locate nearby stations, check bike availability and plan journeys efficiently. With inaccessible station information, struggling to find a convenient bike and navigating the city, the whole experience can become less straightforward, which is less than ideal during busy commutes.

Developed as an individual project for my Mobile Development module, 'Velora' explores how thoughtful interface design and native iOS development can make urban cycling more accessible. I shaped the app concept, designed the user interface and developed the mobile experience in Swift, translating live station data, journey planning and sustainability tracking into an intuitive and cohesive cycling companion.

The Problem

The idea for Velora began during my six-month Erasmus placement in Antwerp, Belgium. Bordering the Netherlands, the city has a strong cycling culture, and I quickly fell in love with how accessible its public transport and bike-sharing systems were. For a one-off €50 payment, I could collect a bike right outside my doorstep and cycle almost everywhere. By the end of my placement, I had completed 385 rides, making cycling an integral part of my everyday life.

Returning to Ireland, I was inspired to try Dublin's public bike-sharing system during a trip to Phoenix Park with a few friends. However, the experience felt considerably less intuitive than what I had grown accustomed to in Belgium. The pricing was expensive and unclear and I accidentally unlocked an electric bike on my first attempt without fully understanding the selection. When it came time to finish my journey, I struggled to locate a nearby station where I could return it.

Having experienced how seamlessly cycling could fit into everyday life abroad, I began questioning why Dublin's equivalent felt so difficult to navigate. Finding nearby stations, understanding bike options and planning journeys should feel straightforward, rather than introducing unnecessary friction into an otherwise convenient and sustainable way to travel.

Our design brief: How might we design an intuitive iOS cycling companion that encourages sustainable urban mobility, improves access to Dublin's bike-sharing network, and supports the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals?

This experience inspired Velora: an iOS cycling companion designed to make Dublin's bike-sharing network easier to understand and use. By bringing station discovery, proximity-based search, journey tracking and sustainability insights into one intuitive interface, I wanted to create an experience that encouraged more people to explore the city by bike.

Research

My research focused on exploring Dublin's existing bike-sharing services and identifying opportunities to improve the overall user experience. I looked at RedClick DublinBikes specifically to understand how the service presents station locations, bike availability and rental options, using my own experience with the system to inform the initial design direction.

To put the project into context, I explored publicly available cycling statistics and developed an initial Figma mock-up to visualise how a more intuitive cycling companion could work:

1.97M DublinBikes journeys were recorded in 2025, highlighting the scale of the city's existing public bike-sharing network.
34% of Dublin Metropolitan Area residents wanted to cycle more in the future, according to the 2025 Walking and Cycling Index.
116 stations serve the DublinBikes network, making clear station discovery and accessible location information important considerations for the mobile experience.

Solution

To address the frustration I experienced with Dublin's bike-sharing platform, I developed Velora, a native iOS cycling companion designed to make finding bikes, navigating stations and tracking journeys more intuitive. Combining a clean interface with station discovery, personalised features and journey management, the app aims to make cycling around Dublin feel more accessible and straightforward.

1. Interactive Map & Station Clustering: The core experience centres around an interactive map displaying nearby bike stations. I implemented MapKit clustering to group stations when zoomed out, reducing visual clutter and rendering overhead. As users zoom in, clusters separate into individual station markers, keeping the map readable while prioritising performance as the volume of displayed data increases.

2. Personalised Station Discovery: Using CoreLocation, I designed a station search experience that prioritises nearby locations, alongside the ability to favourite stations for quicker access. This allows users to keep track of preferred pickup and return points without repeatedly searching the entire network. Clear station information and floating map controls further simplify navigation.

3. Intuitive Journey Tracking: I developed a custom swipe-to-unlock gesture for starting journeys, creating a more deliberate interaction than a standard tap button. The app also tracks completed journeys and displays personal cycling activity through a profile dashboard, including estimated environmental savings. Together, these features connect everyday journey management with a broader focus on sustainable urban travel.

Developed individually in Swift using SwiftUI and UIKit, Velora combined interface design in Figma with native iOS development. A key technical focus was map performance and optimisation, particularly managing station markers and location updates to minimise unnecessary rendering and keep interactions responsive. The result is a cycling companion built around accessible navigation, personalised discovery and a more engaging journey experience.

Reflection

Upon reflection, developing a native iOS application taught me how to balance technical performance with intuitive user experiences. Working with interactive maps, location-based functionality and station data highlighted the importance of optimisation, particularly when handling dense map markers and frequent updates. If there was something I could improve or do differently, I would prioritise prototyping and usability testing earlier in the process, allowing me to gather feedback on station discovery, favouriting and journey tracking before committing to the final implementation.

In the future, I would love to continue developing Velora by refining journey planning, improving station availability insights and expanding notifications around completed rides. I would also explore further accessibility testing and more detailed sustainability metrics to help users understand the environmental impact of their journeys. Overall, this project strengthened my skills in Swift development, UI/UX design and performance optimisation, while teaching me how thoughtful design decisions can make everyday technology more accessible and enjoyable to use.
