Nintendo Switch — UI Redesign

Reimagining the Nintendo Switch interface through clearer navigation, personalised game organisation and a more connected console experience.

PROJECT TYPE Independent UX/UI Case Study
TIMELINE June 2026
ROLE UX Research · UX Design · UI Design
TOOLS Figma · FigJam · Photoshop
DELIVERABLE High-Fidelity Interactive Prototype
PLATFORM Nintendo Switch Concept

Context

The Nintendo Switch has a distinctive interface built around simplicity and getting players into their games quickly. However, I saw opportunities to expand how players organise larger digital libraries, discover new titles and access information about their gaming activity.

I created this independent case study in June to add to my portfolio for my application to Trinity College Dublin, in hopes to pursue an MSc to specialise in Product Design and UX, I then designed this case study specifically to explore how the Nintendo Switch 2 interface could evolve without losing the familiarity that makes the console recognisable. Using Figma, I developed a high-fidelity redesign focused on navigation, personalisation and the overall player experience. The project was a conceptual design exercise, rather than an official Nintendo project or a coded application.

The Problem

I wanted to explore how the console experience could become more personal without making the interface unnecessarily complicated. The existing home screen prioritises recently played games and quick access, which keeps navigation straightforward, but leaves less room for players who want more control over how their libraries and profiles are organised.

I was particularly interested in the gap between finding and launching a game and everything surrounding that experience: managing a growing collection, returning to recently played titles, discovering something new and understanding personal gaming activity.

Rather than adding features for the sake of it, I wanted to consider how these tools could fit naturally into the existing interface. The challenge was to introduce more functionality while preserving Nintendo's approachable visual identity and the speed of controller-based navigation.

My design question: How might the Nintendo Switch interface support a more personalised and organised gaming experience without compromising its simplicity?

Research

I began by examining the existing Nintendo Switch interface and identifying areas I wanted to explore further. I considered its minimal home screen, game navigation and overall visual hierarchy, alongside interface conventions found in other console experiences. This helped me distinguish the elements worth preserving from areas where I felt a redesign could offer more flexibility.

I also collected visual references in an inspiration board to explore different approaches to game artwork, information density, colour and layout. This gave me a clearer direction for developing an interface that felt more structured without becoming visually overwhelming.

My analysis informed three main design priorities: making game organisation more flexible, improving access to relevant player information and creating clearer pathways for discovering content. I used these priorities to guide the screens and interactions I explored in Figma.

Solution

I designed a proposed Nintendo Switch 2 interface that builds on the original console's familiar visual language while exploring more flexible ways to navigate, organise and personalise the gaming experience. I developed the screens in Figma, using consistent layouts and reusable interface elements across light and dark themes.

1. Jump Back In: I introduced a more prominent resume-game area that prioritises the player's current experience. Game artwork, contextual information and a clear primary action help make returning to a title feel more direct.

2. An Expanded Game Library: I explored a more visual library layout to make browsing a collection of games feel organised and engaging. Clearer card layouts and focus indicators were designed with controller navigation in mind, helping the selected item remain easy to identify.

3. Personalised Collections: I proposed custom collections that would let players group games according to their own preferences. This gives players an alternative to relying solely on a chronological list when looking for something to play.

4. Discovery and Recommendations: I explored how recommended titles, upcoming releases and curated content could sit within the wider console experience. The aim was to make discovering games feel more integrated, while keeping the main navigation focused on the player's existing library.

5. A Centralised Player Profile: I redesigned the profile area as a central place to explore player statistics, account information and customisation options. This brought information that could otherwise feel separate into a more cohesive dashboard concept.

Across these screens, I explored light and dark themes, consistent spacing, visual hierarchy and controller focus states. My aim was to make the proposed additions feel like part of one interface system, rather than disconnected features added onto the existing console.

Interactive Figma Prototype

The prototype brings the main screens together so the proposed navigation and interface states can be explored interactively. It demonstrates the direction I would take the experience, rather than a working console implementation.

Reflection

This project gave me the opportunity to look at a familiar digital product from a design perspective and question how its interface could evolve. I learned to think beyond individual screens and consider how navigation, information hierarchy and visual consistency work together across a wider system.

If I developed the project further, I would validate the proposed features with Nintendo Switch users through usability testing. I would want to understand whether custom collections and additional profile information genuinely improve navigation, or whether they introduce unnecessary complexity. I would also test controller focus behaviour and accessibility, particularly text legibility, contrast and consistency across themes.

The outcome is a high-fidelity design concept rather than a tested or implemented product. Although I did not build the interface in code, the project strengthened my ability to analyse an existing experience, develop a visual direction and communicate proposed improvements through an interactive prototype. It also reinforced the importance of treating new features as design decisions that need to justify their place in the user experience.
